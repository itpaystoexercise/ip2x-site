Research Library

The evidence behind IP2X.

IP2X is built on published research — on exercise and longevity, strength training, the power of incentives, and what wearables can reliably measure. This is our working library of the studies and articles that inform the plan. Links open the original sources; where a study isn't freely linkable, we cite it so you can look it up.

Research

Peer-reviewed studies.

  1. Resistance training, aerobic exercise and all-cause mortality
    British Journal of Sports Medicine · 2026

    147,000 people followed up to 30 years: resistance training alone is linked to lower mortality, and combined with aerobic exercise the reduction is greater still.

  2. Muscle-strengthening and aerobic activities and mortality
    The BMJ · 2020

    479,856 participants: aerobic activity cut all-cause mortality substantially, and combining it with resistance training cut it further.

  3. Physical activity, all-cause mortality and longevity
    New England Journal of Medicine · 1986

    ~17,000 people: higher activity in early adulthood and midlife is associated with meaningfully lower all-cause mortality.

  4. Incentives and health behaviour (RAND report RR2870)
    RAND Corporation

    The incentives research behind Vitality-style programmes — loss-based rewards motivate sustained activity more than gain-based ones.

  5. Gamification, financial incentives or both to increase physical activity: the BE ACTIVE randomised trial
    Circulation · American Heart Association

    In patients at high cardiovascular risk, financial incentives increased physical activity over 12 months — with benefits that outlasted the intervention.

  6. Walking and bone mineral density in postmenopausal women
    Bone · ScienceDirect

    Walking did not increase spine bone density; other forms of skeletal loading are needed.

  7. Bone mineral density in swimmers versus high-impact athletes
    PLOS ONE

    Competitive swimmers showed lower bone density than high-impact-sport athletes — closer to sedentary people.

  8. Cycling and peak bone mass in young adults
    PLOS ONE

    Cycling was associated with lower bone mineral content, with a negative effect on peak bone mass.

  9. Swimming, cycling and osteoporosis prevention: a systematic review
    ScienceDirect

    Non-weight-bearing sports such as swimming and cycling are not effective for preventing osteoporosis.

  10. Brisk walking and osteogenic response
    Age and Ageing · Oxford · 1997

    Brisk walking produced a minimal bone-building response, with a greater risk of falls and fracture.

  11. Resistance training to build and preserve muscle
    Scandinavian Journal of Medicine & Science in Sports

    Resistance training is a proven, safe way to build muscle size and strength — effective even in frail individuals and after fracture.

  12. Resistance training for strength and functional recovery
    PubMed Central

    Review of resistance training for building strength and supporting recovery in older and post-fracture populations.

  13. A guide to consumer-grade wearables in cardiovascular care and population health
    Nature (npj) · Michele Orini et al., King's College London

    A non-expert's guide to what consumer wearables can and can't reliably measure in cardiovascular care.

  14. Accuracy of smartwatches for the remote assessment of exercise capacity
    Scientific Reports · Nature

    Validation of Fitbit and Garmin devices for measuring exercise capacity remotely.

  15. Smartwatch estimation of VO2max in free-living activity
    Research Square · preprint

    Performance and limits of smartwatch estimates of cardiorespiratory fitness outside the lab.

  16. Patient-led smartwatch ECG monitoring after AF ablation: a randomised trial
    Journal of the American College of Cardiology

    Using smartwatch ECGs to help patients manage atrial fibrillation after ablation.

  17. Ultra-short heart rate variability and cardiovascular events
    Scientific Reports · Nature

    Long-term association between ultra-short HRV measures and cardiovascular risk.

  18. Premature contractions on wearable-format ECGs and cardiovascular events
    European Heart Journal – Digital Health

    Detecting premature atrial and ventricular contractions on wearable ECGs to predict cardiovascular events.

  19. Validation of wearable heart rate variability and oxygen saturation (Garmin Health Snapshot)
    IEEE

    Validating HRV and oxygen-saturation readings derived from Garmin's Health Snapshot.

  20. Consumer wearables to track sleep in free-living conditions
    IEEE

    Assessing how accurately consumer-grade wearables track sleep in healthy people.

  21. Wearable long-term HRV predicts major adverse cardiovascular events
    IEEE

    In middle-aged people without prior cardiovascular disease, wearable-derived long-term HRV predicts major cardiovascular events.

  22. Wearable monitoring of persistent impairments in long COVID
    Research Square · preprint

    Wearable monitoring detects multi-system impairments in long-COVID cases reporting fatigue years after infection.

  23. Exercise classification in resistance training: a systematic review of technological approaches
    Sports Medicine · Springer

    Identifying resistance-training exercises from wrist-motion sensors is feasible — but not yet proven on consumer wearables.

  24. Leisure-time physical activity and mortality: a meta-analysisCitation
    JAMA Internal Medicine · 2015

    Pooled analysis of ~660,000 people showing the diminishing returns of exercise on mortality. (Referenced by Stephen without a direct link.)

  25. Wearables and short bursts of exercise (VILPA)Citation
    Nature Medicine · 2022

    Sensor study of 25,000 UK adults: a few minutes a day of vigorous incidental activity was linked to markedly lower mortality. (Referenced without a direct link.)

  26. Why "exercise snacks" are good for youCitation
    Sports Medicine and Health Science · 2025

    On the benefits of short, frequent bursts of activity. (Referenced without a direct link.)

  27. Exercise for the treatment of depression: a Cochrane reviewCitation
    Cochrane · 2026

    Pooled 69 randomised trials of exercise for depression. (Referenced via The Economist without a direct link.)

  28. Exercise versus conventional treatments for depression and anxiety: a meta-analysisCitation
    British Journal of Sports Medicine · 2026

    Drew on 1,000+ trials and ~80,000 participants, finding exercise reduces depression and anxiety about as much as conventional treatments. (Referenced without a direct link.)
